# Skolem Problem at Low Orders: Complexity, Derandomization, and Lean 4 Formalization

Computational, number-theoretic, and formal investigation of the **Skolem Problem** for integer Linear Recurrence Sequences (LRS) at low orders ($d \le 4$ and the explicit Order-6 frontier), building on **Bacik, Ouaknine, and Worrell (SODA 2026, `arXiv:2507.11234`)** and the **2026 Survey of the Skolem and Positivity Problems for LRS**.

---

> **Roadmap & status:** see [`PLAN.md`](PLAN.md) for current progress, open problems, and next steps.

## Key Results & Deliverables

1. **Exact Structural Characterization of $\mathsf{EqSLP}$ Instances Generated by `ZeroSearch` ([`notes/eqslp_structure.md`](notes/eqslp_structure.md))**
   - Rather than arbitrary divisions-free Arithmetic Straight-Line Programs (which have doubly-exponential degree $2^{\Theta(s)}$ and encode general Polynomial Identity Testing), the candidate verification step of `arXiv:2507.11234` reduces to testing **at most $M(d-1) < p^d(d-1) = O(1)$ single bilinear matrix-power evaluations**
     $$u_{n_i} = \alpha^\top A^{n_i} \beta, \qquad \alpha, \beta \in \mathbb{Z}^d,\ A \in \mathbb{Z}^{d \times d},\ n_i \le 2^{\mathrm{poly}(\|\boldsymbol{u}\|)},$$
     where every candidate $n_i = M z_i + \ell$ additionally satisfies the perturbed-identity Mahler expansion $A^M = I + p B$ and the $p$-adic disc certificate $u_{n_i} \equiv 0 \pmod{p^R}$ inside a disc where $F_\ell$ has Weierstrass degree $1 \le j \le d-1$.

2. **Reference Implementation & Reproduction of SODA 2026 and the 2026 Survey ([`src/skolem_algorithm.py`](src/skolem_algorithm.py), [`experiments/reproduce_soda2026.py`](experiments/reproduce_soda2026.py))**
   - Complete deterministic implementation of `ZeroSearch` (Algorithm 1 of `arXiv:2507.11234`), including automatic selection of prime $p > d+1$ coprime to $a_0$, multiplicative period $M \le p^d - 1$ with $A^M \equiv I \pmod p$, Mahler difference tables $\Delta^k h_{z,r}(0) \pmod{p^\nu}$ at precision $\nu = V(F_\ell) + r(d-1) + 1$, and $p$-adic disc subdivision up to depth $R$.
   - Exact reproduction of the SODA 2026 worked example ($u_{n+3} = 2u_{n+2} - 3u_{n+1} + u_n$, $u_0=-1, u_1=1, u_2=7$, $p=5, M=8$) and the $17$-adic analysis of the 2026 Survey's explicit open Order-6 sequence $u_n = 2(-4+7i)^n + 2(-4-7i)^n + 4(8+i)^n + 4(8-i)^n + n$.

3. **Deterministic Polynomial-Time Zero-Testing Lemmas & Zero-Bound Classification for Low-Order Subclasses ([`notes/deterministic_zero_testing.md`](notes/deterministic_zero_testing.md), [`src/deterministic_zero_test.py`](src/deterministic_zero_test.py))**
   - **Lemma 1 (2-Root Subclass up to Order 4 — `[Paper: Weil height + Yu 2007]` / `[Lean: conditional wrapper]`)**: Any non-degenerate LRS of order $d \le 4$ with at most $s = 2$ distinct characteristic roots has all zeros bounded by $N_{\text{height}} = O(\|\boldsymbol{u}\|)$ via logarithmic Weil heights (without needing Baker's theorem), and its Bounded Skolem candidates are decided in deterministic $\mathrm{poly}(\|\boldsymbol{u}\|, \log N)$ time by a single prime-power test $u_n \equiv 0 \pmod{p^\nu}$ backed by Yu's $p$-adic Baker bound.
   - **Lemma 2 (Archimedean / MSTV Subclass — `[Paper: Baker–Wüstholz]` / `[Lean: dyadic interval certification]`)**: For any candidate $n$ satisfying the dominant-root separation condition $n \log(|\lambda_1|/|\lambda_{k+1}|) \ge \mathrm{poly}(\|\boldsymbol{u}\|, \log n)$ (including all equi-dominant order-$\le 4$ LRS where $B(n) \equiv 0$), evaluating $u_n = \alpha^\top A^n \beta$ via $O(\log n)$ matrix squarings in $P$-bit dyadic interval arithmetic ($P = \mathrm{poly}(\|\boldsymbol{u}\|, \log n)$) deterministically certifies $u_n = 0$ vs. $u_n \ne 0$.
   - **Global–Local Zero-Bound Classification at Orders 3 and 4 (`notes/deterministic_zero_testing.md` Part IV)**: By normalizing the $q$-adic Newton-polygon slopes of $\tilde{A}^6$ (or $\tilde{A}^{12}$ at order 4) using Bernstein's deterministic polynomial-time Coprime Base Algorithm (`slope_normalize_lrs`) and forming the **Normalized Root-Power Polynomial** $P_\xi(t) = \prod_{i=1}^d (t - \hat{\mu}_i^d/(-\hat{b}_0))$ with coefficient denominator $D$:
     1. **Unit-Root Branch ($k_q = 1$, $D_{k=1} > 1$) — `[Lean: unconditional, zero external hypotheses]`**: Whenever $\chi \equiv (x-\mu)g(x) \pmod{q^{m+1}}$ with $\gcd(\mu, q) = 1$ and all non-leading coefficients of $g$ divisible by $q$ (where $q > 1$ may be an **unfactored composite**, e.g. $q = D_{k=1}$), and $q^{m+1} \nmid u_r$ for some $r < d-1$, every index $k \ge (d-1)(m+1)$ satisfies $q^{m+1} \nmid u_k$ and hence $u_k \ne 0$ (`order3_unit_root_zero_bound`, `order4_unit_root_zero_bound`, `order_d_unit_root_zero_bound`, and across slope normalization `order3_normalized_unit_root_zero_bound`). Every zero has $k = O(\|\boldsymbol{u}\|)$.
     2. **Archimedean Unit Branch ($D = 1$, largest gap at $j \in \{1,2\}$) — `[Paper: Smyth/Boyd + 2-log Baker]` / `[Lean: formal deduction from Baker + gap hypotheses]`**: When $D = 1$, $\xi_1, \dots, \xi_d$ are algebraic units. By Smyth's theorem (order 3) or Kronecker + Smyth/Boyd (order 4), either all moduli are equal ($B(n) \equiv 0$) or the maximum consecutive Archimedean logarithmic gap is at least $\frac{1}{4}\log\theta_0 > 0.0702997$ (order 3) or $\frac{1}{12}\log\theta_0 > 0.023433$ (order 4). When the gap separates 1 vs. 2 roots (always at order 3; $j \in \{1,2\}$ at order 4), 2-logarithm Baker–Wüstholz gives an explicit $K_{\max} = \mathrm{poly}(\|\boldsymbol{u}\|)$ bound on the largest zero (`archimedean_gap_zero_bound`, `order3_unit_branch_smyth_baker_zero_bound`).
     3. **$q$-adic Two-Root Branch ($k_q = 2$ at a prime $q \le \mathrm{poly}(\|\boldsymbol{u}\|)$) — `[Paper: Yu 2007]` / `[Lean: formal deduction from Yu hypothesis]`**: When $q \mid D$ has $k_q = 2$ $q$-dominant roots and $q \le \mathrm{poly}(\|\boldsymbol{u}\|)$, Yu's 1-logarithm $p$-adic bound gives a $\mathrm{poly}(\|\boldsymbol{u}\|)$ zero bound (`padic_two_root_yu_zero_bound`).
     4. **Explicit Residual Open Class — `[Open]`**: Sequences where 3 roots remain nearly equal at $\infty$ while no prime provides a usable 1- or 2-term split: (i) order 4 with $k_\infty = 3$ (e.g., $h_N(x) = x(x-N)^3 - 1$), (ii) $k_q = 3$ for all $q \mid D$, and (iii) order 3/4 with $D_{k=1} = 1$, $\delta_\infty = 2^{-\Omega(\|\boldsymbol{u}\|)}$, and $k_q = 2$ only at superpolynomially large primes $q \mid D$ (constructed explicitly in Step B4 via $g_{N,c}(x) = (x-N)(x^2+x+N^2)-c$, see `notes/deterministic_zero_testing.md` §4.6).

4. **Proven Obstruction Theorems & Explicit Counterexamples ([`notes/deterministic_zero_testing.md`](notes/deterministic_zero_testing.md), [`experiments/counterexamples_and_benchmarks.py`](experiments/counterexamples_and_benchmarks.py))**
   - **Obstruction Theorem B1 (Pisano-Period Defeat of Oblivious Modulus Families — `[Lean]` / `[Paper]`)**: Any oblivious deterministic family of coprime moduli $S = \{m_1, \dots, m_k\}$ of total bit-length $B = \sum_i \log_2 m_i$ fails at the joint Pisano period $n^* = \mathrm{lcm}_{m \in S} \mathrm{ord}_m(A)$ ($\log_2 n^* \le d^2 B$) whenever $u_0 = 0$ and $u_{n^*} \ne 0$.
   - **Obstruction Theorem B2 ($p$-adic Ghost-Root Defeat of Fixed Single-Prime Depth Rules — `[Lean]` / `[Paper]`)**: Whenever a non-dominant subsequence $F_\ell(n) = u_{Mn+\ell}$ has a $p$-adic ghost root $x_0 \in \mathbb{Z}_p \setminus \mathbb{Z}$, its base-$p$ truncations $n_R = M(x_0 \bmod p^R) + \ell$ satisfy $p^{R+1} \mid u_{n_R}$ while $u_{n_R} \ne 0$ (with arbitrarily large valuation jumps whenever a base-$p$ digit of $x_0$ vanishes), defeating fixed single-prime precision rules `(mod p^{R+c})` while being eliminated immediately by Lemma 2's dyadic interval evaluation.
